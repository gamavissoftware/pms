Accessibility — a deliberate decision Stack-light colours cannot be made safe for colour-blind vision by colour alone: green and red sit on the confusion axis for roughly 1 in 12 men. We validated our palette and the worst pair separates by only ΔE 7.6 under deuteranopia. So every state carries an icon and a word, never colour alone, and the flashing tile is reinforced with explicit text. We keep the stack-light colours because your floor already reads them off the machine lights — we just never rely on them by themselves.

Why it is built this way Operators classify stops when it takes one tap, and stop when it takes five. The reason tree is maintained by your supervisor, not by us — so the wording matches what your floor actually says. Entries queue on the tablet and sync if the network drops, and a supervisor can classify any stop missed on the floor from a desk screen later.
What is read automatically, and what is not Automatic from the Mazak control via MTConnect: machine state, controller mode, program number and name, part count, cycle times, spindle load, feed and overrides, axis status, alarm codes with timestamps and duration.

Entered by your people: the downtime reason against an unassigned stop, and the rejected quantity with its reason. Quality cannot come from the control — a CNC does not know what was scrapped — so the Quality factor of OEE depends on that entry. Availability and Performance are fully automatic.

To be confirmed at survey: that each machine publishes part count, and that it increments per part rather than per program cycle. The /probe output from one machine settles this in minutes.
